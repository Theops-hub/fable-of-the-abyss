Last updated: 10 November 2026 (game version 2.15)

In short

Playing requires a connection. It is our server that vouches for every dive: without it neither your pearls nor your record could be guaranteed, and the game would rather not let you play for nothing. What is sent to it is limited to the following:

  • the Pantheon, the online ranking — a name, drawn at random at first launch, which you replace whenever you like, and the depth of your dives;
  • the progress of a dive in play: when it starts, then roughly every ten seconds, the game tells the server how deep you are. This is what allows a record to be validated without taking the device's word for it, and it is also what pauses the run when the server stops answering. These readings are deleted along with the session;
  • today’s sea, friend circles and duels: your depths are ranked there, and seen by those who play them with you;
  • what the server retains of your game: pearl balance, day streak, challenges already rewarded, days on which you played;
  • “Contact us”, the contact form in the settings — nothing is sent until you write;
  • your pearl purchases, whose receipt must be checked with Google or Apple before they are credited.

Two things do not go through our server and go to Google: the rewarded video in the Android and iPhone applications, and the audience measurement in the Android application. Each has its own section below, with what you can refuse.

Who is responsible for your data

The application is published by KFR Studio (Theo Kiefer), the data controller for everything described here. For any question, or to exercise your rights: tk.contact.pro@gmail.com.

Data stored on your device

Your settings, your unlocked fish, the verses of the fable you have already read and a copy of your progress are stored on your device, and disappear if you uninstall the application. The settings never leave it. The rest is also held by our server, and each section of this page says what.

Your pearl balance, in particular, has been held by our server since version 2.5. Your device only keeps a display copy, so the screen tells the truth without waiting for the network — the server's balance is the one that counts.

In a browser, this data uses the site's local storage (localStorage). The browser version sets no cookies and includes neither advertising nor audience measurement: this storage only exists to hand your game back to you at the next launch.

The Pantheon (the online ranking)

At first launch, the game enters you under a name drawn at random (“DIVER4821”) so that you can dive straight away; the end-of-dive screen and the Pantheon then offer to replace it with your own. Every player appears in the ranking — the same mechanism is what lets the server vouch for dives, keep pearls safe, and give your game back if you change phone. The following is sent to our server:

  • the name;
  • your deepest dive and the tier you reached;
  • the fish you set that record with, shown as a small icon next to your name;
  • a random identifier generated when you first take part, and the date you did so;
  • during a dive, depth checkpoints, which let us verify that a record was actually played;
  • with a record, its proof: the duration timed by the server, the number of checkpoints, the shortest and the longest gap between two of them, the starting depth and the record it replaces.

Since version 2.15, the ALL TIME and THIS WEEK rankings count the metres descended in a single dive: for each of them the server keeps your best descent, the depth reached, the starting depth and the fish. The ranking as it stood when 2.15 arrived is frozen under the name “First Sea”: for each player who appeared in it, their depth, the date of that record and their fish.

This identifier is not a hardware identifier: it comes neither from your phone nor from any account, and cannot be used to track you across other applications. It exists solely so that you occupy a single line in the ranking, and so that you can be removed from it.

Your name and score are public by nature, since they appear in the ranking — including in other players' dives, where the record of a neighbour in the ranking serves as a marker to beat. Exchanges with the server are encrypted (HTTPS).

Today’s sea, friend circles and duels

These three modes are rankings smaller than the Pantheon. You only enter them if you play them.

  • Today’s sea — for each day you play it, your deepest dive and the number of your attempts. The top three of a day receive pearls: the server keeps a record of that prize (day, rank, amount).
  • Friend circles — a circle has a name, written by its creator and filtered like a player name, and a six-character code. The server records who is a member of it and since when. The members of a circle see the names and depths of the other members.
  • Duels — a duel has a six-character code. The server records who created it, and the depth of each of those who played it; anyone holding the code sees that ranking. A duel closes seven days after it was created, and its lines are then deleted.

The SHARE button opens your phone's share sheet: the text (a depth, a circle or duel code, a link to the game's store page) goes to the application you choose. We know neither which one, nor whom you send it to.

What the server retains of your game

Several things are held by the server rather than by your phone, for the same reason that applies to pearls: a gain tied to time can be faked by moving the device's clock forward. The following is stored, under your random identifier:

  • your day streak: its length, the last day validated and the number of dives on the current day;
  • the challenges whose reward has already been paid to you, and the date of each payment;
  • the date you last collected from the oyster bed;
  • the number of dives played and the total pearls earned since you arrived;
  • the days on which you played (one line per day, without the time) and the date of your last visit;
  • the platform (Android, iPhone or browser) and the game version you use;
  • three counters against abuse: what your dives have earned in the current hour, the number of “×2 pearls” videos that day, and the date you last created a circle.

The days you visited, the platform and the version let us know how many players come back and which version to notify of an update. It is a measurement kept by our server alone: it is passed on to no one.

“Contact us” (contact form, optional)

The Settings screen offers a form to report a problem or to ask us to act on your game. If you use it, we receive:

  • the text of your message and the subject you picked (bug, account, other);
  • your random identifier and the associated name;
  • a little technical information attached automatically, so that we can understand the problem without having to ask: the game version, the platform, the display language, your deepest dive and your pearl balance.

Do not write anything in that message you would rather not share with us: its content is free-form, and you are the one writing it. We ask for neither your name, nor your e-mail address, nor any other contact details. If we reply to you, the reply is shown in the game, at your next launch; it is kept on our server with your message.

When a message arrives, our server notifies us by e-mail. That e-mail contains your name, the beginning of your identifier, your text and the technical information above. It is carried by Resend, an e-mail delivery provider established in the United States, to our inbox (Gmail).

Messages are read by the publisher. They are kept, together with our replies, to follow up your request: no automatic deletion is scheduled, and they are deleted if you ask for your data to be deleted.

Your pearls and your purchases

Since version 2.5, your pearl balance is held by our server rather than by your device alone. Two reasons: giving your pearls back when you change phone or reinstall the game, and preventing anyone from granting themselves for free what is being sold. We therefore store your random identifier, your balance, and the operations that moved it outside of dives (a spend, a refund). These operations are deleted automatically after 92 days, with two exceptions, kept as long as your game exists because they explain a disputed balance: the carry-over of your original balance, and any corrections the publisher may have made by hand.

If you buy a pearl pack, the game sends our server the receipt issued by your device's store, and our server verifies it before crediting anything. What travels is not quite the same from one store to the other:

  • on Android, Google Play issues a purchase token, the signed description of the transaction, and its signature; our server checks that signature against the application's public key;
  • on iPhone, the App Store issues a transaction identifier, and nothing else. Our server then asks Apple directly whether that purchase really took place — and has not been refunded since.

In both cases we keep that receipt, the pack concerned, the number of pearls credited, the store and the date: this is what prevents the same purchase from being credited twice, and what lets us find your payment again if something went wrong. We receive neither your name, nor your address, nor your payment method: the transaction itself takes place entirely at Google (its policy) or at Apple (its own), under theirs.

Moving to a new phone

Settings → Move to a new phone issues an eight-character code, valid for thirty minutes and usable once. During that time we keep the code, the identifier of the player it grants access to and the list of that player's fish, to give them back on the new device; afterwards the code is worthless. Entering it on a second device attaches that device to the same game: we then store its random identifier alongside the first.

Reminders (optional)

After your third dive, the Android and iPhone applications ask you once whether they may send you reminders (“Today’s sea has changed”, “Your streak is waiting”). These notifications are scheduled by your phone itself: nothing is sent to our server or to a notification service, and we receive no identifier of your device for this. At most one goes out per day, for at most three days after you last opened the game. You can turn them off at any time in Settings → Reminders.

Rewarded advertising

A single form of advertising exists in the game, and only in the Android and iPhone applications: the so-called “rewarded” video. It is offered in two places on the end-of-dive screen: to resume the dive (once per dive, never in today’s sea or in a duel), and to double the pearls the dive earned (ten times a day at most). It only plays if you tap the button — never forced, never between screens, no banner, no interstitial. The browser version of the game shows no advertising at all.

These videos are served by Google AdMob. Where European regulations apply, a consent form appears before the very first one, after one of your first dives: you can consent, fine-tune (“Manage options”), or refuse. If you refuse, the video button simply never appears, and the game remains fully playable — the choice costs nothing but what the video would have offered.

On iPhone, a second prompt follows the first, and it comes from Apple rather than from us: the “Allow “Fable of the Abyss” to track your activity” alert. That one decides whether your device's advertising identifier may be read. Declining takes nothing away from the game or the video — the ads are simply less targeted. You can revisit this at any time in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking.

To display an ad and guard against fraud, Google processes data directly from your device, to the extent your choice allows: the advertising identifier, the IP address, information about the device, and how you interact with the ad. None of it passes through our server or is shared with us. All we learn, from your own device, is that the video was watched to the end, so we can give the dive back or double your pearls; the server counts the day's doublings. What Google does is detailed in its privacy policy and in the list of partners shown by the form.

Changing your mind can be done at any time, inside the game: Settings → Ads: your choices reopens the form. Your choice is stored on your device, not with us.

Audience measurement (Android application)

The Android application includes Google Analytics for Firebase. It lets us know whether the game works: where dives end, which screens are opened, whether one version retains players better than the previous one. The iPhone application and the browser version do not include it.

What is sent to Google, from your device:

  • game events: start and end of a dive (depth, duration, cause of death, fish played), tiers crossed, bubbles earned, challenges completed, encounters, verses read;
  • screen events: shop or ranking opened, fish chosen, share started, duel created or joined, reminders accepted or refused, rating window requested;
  • the pearls earned and spent, the videos offered, watched or declined (with the revenue Google attributes to us for each), and the purchases started, completed or abandoned (the pack and its displayed price);
  • characteristics of your game, sorted into bands: number of dives, deepest dive, number of fish, most-played fish, week of installation, purchase already made or not;
  • what Google's tool records on its own: the device model, the Android version, the language, the game version, and the country and city it infers from your IP address.

None of these events carries your name, your player identifier, the code of a circle or a duel, or the text of a message.

What your answer to the form changes. The consent form of the previous section also applies to the measurement:

  • until you have answered, and if you refuse, the events are sent all the same, but without an identifier: Google can neither tie them to your device, nor link them to one another from one day to the next;
  • if you accept, Google assigns your installation an identifier of its own — it changes if you reinstall the game — and may read the device's advertising identifier if you have also accepted advertising;
  • where European regulations do not apply, Google does not show this form and the measurement runs with an identifier.

You can go back on this choice in Settings → Ads: your choices.

Where this data goes, and for how long. Google keeps it on our behalf, and may process it in the United States, under the data protection framework agreed between the European Union and the United States (Data Privacy Framework). In Google Analytics, events tied to an identifier are deleted after fourteen months at most. We receive a daily copy of them in Google BigQuery, hosted in the European Union: it contains no advertising identifier. No automatic deletion is scheduled there — we keep it for as long as the game is published, to compare its versions with one another.

What we do not collect

Beyond what is described on this page, no data is sent to our server: no e-mail address, no contacts, no location, no hardware identifier. Our database records no IP address; our host sees it pass by, as any web server does, and keeps a technical log of requests for a few days, which we only consult in the event of a failure. Your device's advertising identifier never reaches us: only Google uses it, with your consent, for the rewarded video and the measurement described above. The window that invites you to rate the game is the store's own: we do not know whether you rated it.

Where your data is, and who can access it

The server and database are hosted by Cloudflare (D1 service), in its Western Europe region. Cloudflare acts as a processor: it hosts, and does nothing else with your data. As a US company, its technical teams may access it to operate the service; such access is covered by the European Commission's standard contractual clauses.

Other companies receive data, each for a specific use:

  • Google, for the rewarded video (AdMob, Android and iPhone) and audience measurement (Analytics for Firebase, Android) — directly from your device, within the limits of your choice;
  • Resend, to carry the e-mail that notifies us of your message, if you write to us;
  • Google Play or Apple, which our server queries to verify a purchase.

Apart from that, no data held by our server is sold, rented, or passed to any third party, for advertising, commercial or other purposes.

Your rights

You have the right to access, rectify, erase, restrict and object to the processing of your data, as well as a right to portability. The simplest way to exercise them is described just below, under “Deleting your data” — the same route works for any request, and your identifier is attached automatically.

If our answer does not satisfy you, you may lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority: in France, the CNIL (www.cnil.fr).

Automated decisions

Three mechanisms run without human intervention, and it is only fair to say so: player names and circle names are filtered automatically to rule out insulting terms, a score judged physically impossible is refused by the server, and the pearls earned in one hour are capped at what a very good player can collect. None has any effect outside the game, and you can dispute each of them by writing to us: a human will look.

Deleting your data

This section concerns the application “Fable of the Abyss”, published by KFR Studio (tk.contact.pro@gmail.com).

How to request deletion

Two ways, whichever you prefer:

  1. From the game — open Settings, tap “CONTACT US”, choose the subject “MY GAME” and ask for your data to be deleted. Your identifier is attached automatically: we can find your entry without you having to describe it, and without you having to give us anything else.
  2. By e-mail — write to tk.contact.pro@gmail.com, stating your player name.

Either way, deletion is carried out within 30 days at most.

Data that is deleted

Deletion erases all the data concerning you on our server:

  • your name;
  • your deepest dive, its proof, the tier you reached, and your place in the “First Sea”;
  • the random identifier of each of your installations, and the date you first took part;
  • your lines in the rankings of the week, of today’s sea and of duels, as well as the duels you created;
  • your memberships of circles, and the circles of which you were the only member;
  • your streak, your rewarded challenges, your sea of the day prizes, the days you visited and all the counters of your game;
  • any dive sessions still open;
  • your pearl balance and its operations;
  • the change-of-phone code, if one existed;
  • any messages you may have sent us, our replies, and the alert e-mail left in our inbox.

Two things remain, and no longer identify you:

  • the receipt of any purchase, whose link to you is erased at the same moment. The receipt alone, with no owner, is what prevents an already-paid purchase from being submitted again to be credited a second time; it is also the accounting proof of the transaction, which we are required to keep;
  • a circle you had created and in which other players remain: it belongs to them too. It stays, without your name.

Apart from them, no data concerning you is kept on our server, and we retain no copy for any other purpose.

Measurement events (Android)

The events sent to Google Analytics carry neither your name nor your player identifier: we therefore cannot find yours from your request. Two actions are in your hands. Refusing in Settings → Ads: your choices makes subsequent events go out without an identifier. Uninstalling the game, or clearing its data from Android's settings, breaks the link with the identifier Google had assigned to your installation; the events already tied to that identifier are deleted from Google Analytics within the period indicated above.

Residual retention

Our host (Cloudflare D1, Western Europe) makes automatic technical backups so the database can be restored after an incident. These backups are kept for 30 days at most, then destroyed: after that, no trace of your data remains on our server. They are never consulted or used outside of a restoration following a technical incident.

Data remaining on your phone

Your settings, your unlocked fish and the copy of your progress are stored locally on your device. They are deleted when you uninstall the application, or when you clear its data from your phone's settings. Your pearl balance, however, is held by our server (see “Your pearls and your purchases”): the copy kept on your phone disappears with the rest, but it is the deletion request above that erases the balance itself.

Moderation

Player names and circle names are filtered automatically to rule out insulting or hateful terms. An inappropriate name can be reported from Settings → Contact us or by e-mail, and will be removed.

Children

The application is intended for players aged 13 and over — not least because it offers purchases and advertising. As the Pantheon is public, we encourage younger players never to use their real name as their player name or as a circle name, and not to write personal information in the contact form.
